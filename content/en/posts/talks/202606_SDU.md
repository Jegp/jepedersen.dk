A talk for SDU Microelectronics on why getting efficiency out of unconventional substrates &mdash; neuromorphic, analog, photonic &mdash; means closing the *representational gap* between classical computer-science nomenclature and the physics that actually runs the computation.

The talk covers (1) the representational gap between abstract models and physical substrates (Horsman, 2014); (2) two ways to close it &mdash; aligning computation with the physics, and exploiting the sparsity of the signal rather than computing every path; (3) the Neuromorphic Intermediate Representation (NIR) as a cross-platform, substrate-faithful compilation target ([Nature Communications, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-52259-9)); (4) Exp-Minus-Log as an example of a *continuous* calculus; (5) asynchronous signal processing that is realizable directly in physical media &mdash; covariant spatio-temporal receptive fields ([Nature Communications, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-63493-0)) and spiking-wavelet ADCs; and (6) the path from there toward physically-faithful AI accelerators.
