Event cameras and spiking hardware promise microsecond latency and milliwatt budgets, but the pipelines that consume their output are built by hand: filters chosen empirically, with no principled answer to whether the sparse spike stream still carries the signal, or how features behave when the scene moves or rescales. The talk covers (1) what a *frame* means in signal processing, and why it is not a video frame; (2) the field's own diagnosis that models and theories need to be co-designed with hardware; (3) two guarantees that transfer conventional signal processing to event-driven systems &mdash; spiking wavelet frames, whose bounds mean the signal is stably recoverable from spikes ([arXiv:2602.02020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02020)), and covariant spatio-temporal receptive fields, so features respond predictably under motion and rescaling ([Nature Communications, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-63493-0)); and (4) compiling such pipelines to FPGA through the Neuromorphic Intermediate Representation ([Nature Communications, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-52259-9)).
